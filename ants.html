How to Get Rid of Sugar Ants in the Kitchen

Homeowners often call any small ant in the kitchen a sugar ant. The name is informal. The ants you are seeing may be odorous house ants, Argentine ants, pavement ants, or another species that is common in your area. What they share is a habit of following a scent trail from a nest to something sweet, greasy, or wet on a counter, in a cabinet, or around a pet bowl.

Getting rid of them is less about chasing every ant you see and more about breaking that trail, removing what they came for, and sending a slow-acting bait back to the nest. If you only spray the workers on the counter, the colony usually stays in place and new foragers return.

How to tell what you are dealing with

Watch one ant instead of wiping the first one you see. Workers often travel in a thin line along a backsplash, the edge of a cabinet, a windowsill, or the underside of a counter. Follow the line toward the entry point. That is usually a hairline crack, a gap around a pipe, a window frame, or the gap under a dishwasher or refrigerator.

Note what they are feeding on. Some ants prefer sweets such as juice, syrup, or a sticky residue on a jar. Others will take grease, pet food, or crumbs. That detail helps you choose a bait they will actually carry home. Also check under the sink, around the dishwasher, and near houseplants. Standing water and damp wood can be as attractive as food.

A small number of ants after a spill can be a short visit from an outdoor nest. A line of ants that returns every day, especially in more than one room, points to a colony that has a reliable route into the house.

What you can do yourself

Wipe the trail and remove the food

Wash counters, the stovetop, and cabinet edges with soapy water. Soap breaks up the scent trail that other workers follow. Empty the trash often, rinse recyclables, and wipe the floor around the trash can and pet bowls. Store sugar, cereal, honey, snacks, and pet food in containers with tight lids. Do not leave dirty dishes in the sink overnight, and wipe up spills as soon as you can.

Pull the stove and refrigerator out far enough to sweep and mop the floor underneath. Crumbs and grease in those gaps are a regular food source that people miss. Check the drip tray and the seals on the fridge if ants are clustered there.

Find the entry and seal it

After you have followed the trail, seal the gap with caulk, a door sweep, or a tight escutcheon around a pipe. Outdoors, trim vegetation that touches the house, and move woodpiles and dense mulch a short distance away from the foundation. Ants often use those hidden pathways to reach a kitchen wall.

Use bait, not a quick spray, as your main tool

Place a slow-acting ant bait near the trail, but not on top of a food prep surface. Workers need time to find it, feed, and take it back to the nest, including the queen. If the ants ignore a sweet bait, try a protein or grease bait. Do not spray around the bait, and do not wipe the trail immediately next to it, or the workers may never find it.

Keep children and pets from reaching the bait. Read the product label and use only what the label allows indoors. If ants seem to vanish for a day and then return in a new spot, they may have switched food sources. Clean again and move the bait to the new trail rather than applying more product in more rooms.

Outdoor nests sometimes sit under a sidewalk, in a lawn, or along the foundation. Baiting the indoor trail still helps, because the same workers are going back and forth. Sealing the entry makes it harder for a new trail to form after the colony declines.

When to call a professional

A kitchen trail that you can clean, seal, and bait is often a job you can handle. Frequently asked questions

Why do sugar ants keep coming back to my kitchen?

They are following a scent trail to crumbs, spills, pet food, or a leak. Wiping the trail and removing the food or water source matters more than killing the ants you see on the counter.

Should I spray the ants I see?

A spray may kill a few workers, but it often leaves the colony in place. For some species, spraying can also split one colony into several. A slow-acting bait that workers carry home is usually more effective.

How long does bait take to work?

You may still see ants for several days while they feed and share the bait. Leave the bait in place, keep other food wiped up, and avoid spraying around it.

When should I call a pest control provider for ants?

Call if ants keep returning after you have cleaned, sealed entry points, and used bait for a week or more, or if you think they are nesting inside a wall or under a sink.
