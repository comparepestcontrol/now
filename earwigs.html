How to Get Rid of Earwigs in the House

Earwigs are easy to recognize once you know the feature that gives them their name in casual talk: a pair of forceps, or pincers, at the end of the body. They are usually reddish brown, about half an inch to an inch long, and they hide in tight, damp places during the day. At night they wander in search of food. Despite the old story, they do not seek out human ears. They can pinch if you handle them, so it is better to sweep or vacuum them than to pick them up.

Indoors, earwigs are almost always visitors from the yard. They live in mulch, leaf litter, under flowerpots, in firewood, and along a damp foundation. A wet week, heavy irrigation, or a tight row of mulch against the house can push them under a door and into a kitchen, bathroom, or basement.

How to tell where they are coming from

If you see earwigs on a windowsill, in a bathtub, or along a sliding-door track, look at the outdoor side of that same wall. Lift the edge of the mulch, check under pot saucers, and look around an outdoor faucet or downspout. Earwigs gather where it stays moist and dark. You may also find them in a damp bathroom or laundry room, because those rooms offer the same conditions.

A few earwigs after rain can be incidental. Regular indoor sightings, especially in the same room, mean there is a reliable path and a damp harborage just outside or under the house. Check door sweeps, the bottom of a garage door, torn screens, and the track of a patio door, which can hold both moisture and insects.

What you can do yourself

Dry the foundation and clean up hiding places

Pull mulch several inches back from the siding and keep it from staying soaked. Clean leaf litter and weeds from the foundation. Raise flowerpots on stands and empty saucers so water does not sit under them. Store firewood off the ground and away from the house. Fix downspouts and grading so water runs away from the wall instead of pooling along it. If you water the garden, avoid soaking the strip right against the house late in the day.

Indoors, fix leaks, dry bathroom and basement floors, and reduce clutter in the rooms where you have seen earwigs. Vacuum or sweep up the insects you find and empty the vacuum outside.

Seal entries and use simple traps

Add or replace door sweeps, repair screens, and clean patio-door tracks. Caulk obvious gaps around pipes and windows. Reduce bright lights at entry doors at night, because lights draw other insects, and earwigs follow shelter and prey toward the same threshold.

Outdoors, a shallow trap can confirm the source. A rolled-up, slightly damp newspaper or a short length of hose left along the foundation overnight will often collect earwigs by morning. Shake the trap into a bucket of soapy water. Sticky monitors indoors, placed along the wall near the door they use, will show whether the number of visitors is falling after you have dried and sealed the area.

A labeled outdoor treatment along the foundation is optional and should follow the product directions. It is not a substitute for removing the wet mulch and the gap under the door. If you do not want to use a pesticide, the moisture and exclusion steps are still the core of the work.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if earwigs keep coming indoors after you have thinned mulch, improved drainage, and sealed doors, or if you find large numbers in a crawl space or along many walls at once. A technician can inspect the exterior, identify hidden harborage, and treat the foundation area if it is appropriate for your property.

It is worth mentioning any other damp-weather visitors, such as millipedes, crickets, or house centipedes. The same foundation conditions often support all of them.

Frequently asked questions

Do earwigs pinch or live in human ears?

Earwigs can pinch with the forceps at the end of the body, but they do not seek out human ears. Indoors they are looking for moisture and a dark place to hide during the day.

Why do earwigs appear after rain or watering?

They live in damp mulch, leaf litter, and tight outdoor spaces. After rain or heavy watering they often move to new shelter, and a gap under a door or around a sliding door can lead them inside.

How do I keep earwigs out of the house?

Thin the mulch at the foundation, fix drainage, seal door gaps, reduce outdoor lights at entries, and trap the insects that are already hiding in damp corners.

When should I call a professional for earwigs?

Call if large numbers keep coming indoors after you have dried the foundation area and sealed doors, or if you cannot find the source along the exterior.
