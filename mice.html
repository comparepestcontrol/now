Who to Call for Mice in the House

If you are asking who to call for mice in the house, you have probably already heard scratching at night, found droppings in a cupboard, or noticed chewed food packaging. A house mouse can squeeze through a gap about the size of a pencil, and one small opening at a pipe, vent, or door bottom is enough to start a problem.

You do not always need a technician on the first day. A light, recent problem in one room can often be handled with careful trapping and sealing. You should call a licensed pest control provider when the activity is widespread, when you cannot find the entry, or when trapping does not slow things down. Signs you have mice in the house

House mouse droppings are small and pointed, about the size of a grain of rice. You may find them along baseboards, in cabinets, behind the stove, or in a garage or pantry. Other signs include gnaw marks on wood or plastic, shredded paper or packaging used for nesting, greasy rub marks along a frequently used run, and scratching in walls or ceilings after dark.

Mice usually travel along edges rather than across open floors. If you put a thin layer of flour or talc along a baseboard in the evening, you may see tracks by morning. Check the attic, basement, and the space under a kitchen sink as well. Food and warmth draw them into living areas, but the nest may be in a wall, a cluttered closet, or an appliance gap.

If the droppings are much larger, or you see damage that looks too big for a mouse, do not assume it is the same problem. The cleanup and sealing steps below are still useful, but a technician should identify the animal before you choose a method.

What you can do yourself

Seal the house

Walk the outside and inside of the house looking for gaps around pipes, cable lines, dryer vents, garage doors, and the bottoms of exterior doors. Stuff copper mesh or steel wool into small holes and seal over it with caulk or a repair filler rated for the material. Add door sweeps. Repair torn screens and loose vent covers. Inside, close gaps under kitchen and bathroom sinks where pipes come through the wall.

Store food, bird seed, and pet food in sturdy containers with tight lids. Wipe crumbs, empty trash, and do not leave pet food out overnight. Mice only need a small amount of food, so a pantry bag with a torn corner can keep a problem going.

Trap along the walls

Use several snap traps, not just one. Place them along the wall, with the trigger side facing the wall, in the areas where you found droppings or rub marks. Peanut butter or a small smear of seed works as bait. Check traps daily. Wear gloves when you handle used traps and when you clean droppings. Ventilate the room, avoid stirring dust, and wet-wipe droppings rather than sweeping them dry.

Skip glue boards as your main method if you can. They are harder to handle humanely and often catch fewer mice than well-placed snap traps. Poison baits can also leave a mouse dead in a wall, and they are a risk in homes with children or pets. If you are not comfortable trapping, that is a sound reason to call a provider rather than putting out poison.

When to call a professional

Call a licensed residential pest control provider if you find droppings in several rooms, hear activity in walls or the attic for more than a few nights, catch mice for a week without a pause, or cannot locate the entry. Also call if someone in the home has a breathing illness, or if you would rather not handle traps and cleanup yourself.

A technician should inspect for entries, set traps or other devices in hidden runways, and explain what sealing you or a contractor still need to finish. Exclusion is what keeps mice from coming back after the current animals are gone. Ask whether follow-up visits are part of the plan, and confirm that the company is licensed and insured for rodent work in your state.

Frequently asked questions

Who should I call if I have mice in the house?

Call a licensed residential pest control provider that handles rodent work in your area. Compare Pest Control can connect you with an independent local company at 844-609-3439. You can also start with traps and sealing if you have only seen a few signs.

How do I know it is mice and not another animal?

House mice leave rice-sized droppings, gnaw small holes in food packaging, and often scratch in walls or ceilings at night. Larger droppings, louder noises, or outdoor burrows can point to a different problem that needs a different plan.

Can I handle a mouse problem myself?

A light problem, such as a few droppings in one room, can often be handled with snap traps and sealing holes as small as a quarter inch. If you hear activity in many rooms, find a lot of droppings, or cannot find the entry, call a provider.

Is poison the first step for mice in a house?

Poison is a poor first step in most homes. A mouse may die in a wall, and bait can be a risk to children and pets. Snap traps and exclusion are usually the safer starting point.
