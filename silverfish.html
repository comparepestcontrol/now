How to Get Rid of Silverfish in the Bathroom

Silverfish are the small, carrot-shaped, silver-gray insects that dart along a bathroom baseboard or end up in a sink or tub. They have long antennae and three bristles at the tail end. They move quickly, avoid light, and hide in cracks during the day. Firebrats look similar but prefer hotter, drier areas such as a water-heater closet. In bathrooms, silverfish are the usual visitor.

They come for moisture first, then for starch and protein: toothpaste residue, paper, cardboard, glue, book bindings, and sometimes fabric. Getting rid of them in the bathroom starts with drying the room out. Traps and careful product use can reduce the ones that are already hiding in the walls or cabinets, but a damp room will keep bringing them back.

How to tell if silverfish are established

A single silverfish in the tub can be a wanderer that slipped on the smooth surface and could not climb out. Repeated sightings, especially at night when you turn on the light, point to a hiding place nearby: under the vanity, behind the toilet, around the tub surround, or in a linen closet that shares a wall with the bathroom.

Look for small yellow stains, tiny dark droppings, or shed skins in the corners of cabinets and on stacked paper or stored towels. Check other damp rooms as well, including a laundry room, basement bathroom, or kitchen sink cabinet. Silverfish can live in more than one humid pocket in the same house.

They are not a sign of poor housekeeping on their own. Older houses with hidden leaks, bathrooms with no exhaust fan, and tightly sealed new homes that trap humidity can all support them. The goal is to make the room too dry and too bare for them to stay.

What you can do yourself

Cut the moisture

Run the exhaust fan during showers and for at least twenty minutes afterward. If the bathroom has no fan, open a window or use a portable dehumidifier in an adjoining space. Fix leaking toilets, tub seals, and supply valves. Dry the floor, the sink, and the shower walls after use, and hang towels so they dry fully. A slow leak under the vanity is a classic hiding place, so look with a flashlight and feel for soft drywall or a damp cabinet bottom.

Aim for indoor humidity that stays comfortable and not steamy for long periods. In a basement bathroom, a dehumidifier for the whole level may be necessary. Keep lids on the toilet and do not store wet washcloths in a tight pile.

Reduce food and hiding places, then trap

Move stacks of magazines, cardboard, and unused paper out of the bathroom. Store extra toilet paper and towels in a dry hall closet if the bathroom stays humid. Vacuum baseboards, cabinet corners, and the gap behind the toilet, then empty the vacuum outside. Sticky monitors along the wall behind the toilet and under the vanity will show you whether activity is falling.

If you use a bait or a crack-and-crevice product labeled for silverfish, follow the label and keep it away from children, pets, and toiletries. Do not spray the entire room. The insects hide in tight gaps, so a wide spray on open tile does little. Give drying and trapping a couple of weeks before you add more product.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if silverfish keep appearing after you have fixed leaks, improved ventilation, and used monitors for two weeks or more. Also call if you find them in closets, around books, or in several rooms, or if you suspect a hidden plumbing leak but cannot find it. A technician can inspect wall voids and other humid areas, treat cracks more thoroughly, and help you decide whether the bathroom is the only source.

Mention any other pests you have seen. Moisture problems that support silverfish can also support cockroaches or house centipedes, and it is more efficient to deal with the shared cause.

Frequently asked questions

Why are there silverfish in my bathroom?

Silverfish look for humid rooms and quiet cracks. A bathroom with a slow leak, poor ventilation, or damp towels gives them water and hiding places, and they will also feed on paper, glue, and leftover toiletries.

Do silverfish bite or damage much?

They do not bite people, but they can stain or nibble paper, book bindings, wallpaper paste, and some fabrics. In a bathroom you may notice them more than you notice the damage.

Will a dehumidifier help?

Yes, if the room or the adjoining basement stays damp. Silverfish need moisture. Running an exhaust fan, fixing leaks, and keeping indoor humidity lower are the most useful long-term steps.

When should I call a professional for silverfish?

Call if you still see them after the bathroom is dry and clutter is reduced, or if you find them in many rooms, in a closet of clothes, or around books and papers you want to protect.
