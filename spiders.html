How to Get Rid of Spiders in the House

Seeing a spider on the ceiling or in a bathtub is common, especially in late summer and fall. Most spiders in a house are there because they found insects to eat and a quiet corner to wait. Getting rid of them is mainly about making the house less useful as a hunting ground, then removing the spiders and webs that are already inside.

You do not need to treat every spider as an emergency. Many household species help reduce flies and other insects. Still, it is reasonable to want fewer of them in living rooms, bedrooms, and children's rooms, and to get help if you are unsure what you are seeing.

What the spiders are telling you

Webs in upper corners, around windows, and along a porch suggest web-building spiders that stay put if the spot keeps producing prey. Spiders that run across floors or hide in closets and shoes are often hunters. A sudden increase in either type can mean there are more insects in the house or around the foundation than you had noticed. Damp basements, bright outdoor lights, and cluttered storage all support that food chain.

Look also at entry points. Gaps under doors, loose screens, and open vents let both insects and spiders in. In bathrooms, a spider in the tub often wandered in during the night and cannot climb back out of the smooth surface. That is a sign they are already in the room, not that they came through the drain.

If you are concerned about a specific spider, take a clear photo from a safe distance. Identification is more useful than a close encounter. Outdoor woodpiles, sheds, and undisturbed storage are more likely places to find species that warrant extra care. Indoors, most sightings are ordinary house spiders.

What you can do yourself

Remove webs, clutter, and prey

Vacuum webs, egg sacs, and the corners where walls meet ceilings. Empty the vacuum bag or canister outside. Go over window frames, closet corners, basement joists, and the garage as well. Reduce cardboard piles, unused shoes left on the floor, and stacks of unused fabric. Spiders rest in those still spaces.

Work on the insects they eat at the same time. Keep kitchen counters clean, fix leaks, and use tight lids on trash. If you have a known problem with cockroaches, crickets, or other indoor insects, deal with that source. A house with fewer prey insects usually has fewer spiders.

Seal and change the lighting

Add door sweeps, repair screens, and caulk gaps around windows and utility lines. Outdoors, move firewood, dense plantings, and piles of stored items away from the foundation when you can. Bright white lights at the door attract flying insects at night, and spiders follow. A lower, warmer porch light, or a light placed farther from the door, often reduces both.

Sticky monitors along baseboards in a garage, basement, or storage room can catch wandering spiders and show you which rooms are most active. Check them on a schedule. A household insecticide spray is optional and, if you use one, should be limited to cracks listed on the label. It will not replace cleaning and sealing.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if spiders keep appearing after you have vacuumed and sealed, if many spiders appear in a short time, or if you want help identifying a spider that concerns you. A technician can inspect the foundation, attic, and crawl space, look for the insects that are feeding the spiders, and suggest a focused plan rather than treating every room the same way.

This is also a good time to mention other pests you have seen. House centipedes, crickets, and cockroaches can all support a higher spider population. Solving those issues often solves the spider issue with them.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I suddenly have spiders in the house?

Spiders go where their prey goes. A rise in indoor insects, a change in weather, or clutter that gives them quiet corners can all lead to more sightings. Reducing other pests and hiding places usually reduces spiders as well.

Are house spiders dangerous?

Most spiders found in U.S. homes are not a serious medical concern. A few species can bite in self-defense. If you cannot identify a spider and are worried about it, capture a clear photo and call a local provider rather than handling it.

Does spraying every corner get rid of spiders?

A spray may kill the spider you see, but it does not remove the insects they were hunting or the gaps they used to enter. Vacuuming, sealing, and reducing outdoor lighting usually have a longer effect.

When should I call a professional about spiders?

Call if you see many spiders in a short period, if webs keep returning in the same rooms, or if you think you have found a species that concerns you and want a trained person to inspect the house.
