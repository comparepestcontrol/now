How to Get Rid of Crickets in the House

A cricket in the house is often heard before it is seen. House crickets chirp from a hidden spot along a baseboard or behind a box. Camel crickets, also called spider crickets or cave crickets, usually do not sing. They have a humped back and long legs, and they startle people by jumping across a basement floor. Both kinds come indoors for moisture, cool shelter, and the insects or scraps they can find there.

Getting rid of them is mostly an exclusion and moisture job. If the basement, garage, or crawl space stays damp and the door gaps stay open, new crickets will replace the ones you catch.

Where crickets hide and how they get in

Start in the lowest, darkest rooms. Check along foundation walls, behind stored boxes, near floor drains, in laundry rooms, and in crawl-space entries. House crickets may also wander into kitchens and living rooms, especially at night or when lights are on. Outdoors, they live in mulch, weeds, firewood, and the thatch or debris along the foundation.

Entry points are often simple: a garage door that does not seal, a missing door sweep, a torn screen, a gap around a utility pipe, or a crawl-space vent with a damaged cover. Bright lights at the back door or garage can draw crickets to the threshold, and then a small gap lets them through.

A few crickets after a stretch of hot, dry weather or after a heavy rain can be seasonal. Crickets that you hear or see for weeks, or that appear in large numbers in one room, mean the house is offering them a steady place to live.

What you can do yourself

Dry the room and clear the edges

Fix leaks, run a dehumidifier in a damp basement, and use exhaust fans in laundry rooms. Move cardboard boxes off the floor and onto shelves. Reduce newspaper piles and other clutter that gives crickets cover. Outdoors, pull mulch and leaf litter a short distance back from the foundation, mow tall weeds, and store firewood away from the wall.

Change door lighting if you can. A porch light that stays on all night will keep drawing insects to the doorway. A motion light, a lower-wattage warmer light, or a fixture placed away from the door can reduce that traffic.

Seal and trap

Install door sweeps, repair screens, and caulk gaps around pipes and window frames. Make sure the garage door seal touches the floor along its full width. Repair crawl-space doors and vents so they close tightly.

Catch the crickets already indoors with sticky monitors along walls and in corners, or with a simple container trap in a basement corner. Vacuum the ones you see and empty the vacuum outside. If you use a product labeled for crickets, apply it only where the label allows, such as cracks along a foundation, and keep it away from children and pets. A wide indoor spray is rarely necessary if you have dried and sealed the space.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if crickets keep appearing after you have reduced moisture and sealed obvious gaps, if a crawl space is too tight or unsafe for you to work in, or if the chirping is coming from inside a wall and you cannot find the insects. A technician can inspect the foundation and crawl space, treat exterior harborage, and point out entries that are easy to miss.

Mention other moisture-loving pests you have seen, such as earwigs, silverfish, or house centipedes. Those sightings often share the same damp conditions, and it is worth inspecting for all of them at once.

Frequently asked questions

Why are crickets in my house?

Crickets come indoors for moisture, cool shelter, and food scraps, and they are often drawn toward lights at night. Basements, garages, and crawl spaces are the rooms they find first.

What is the difference between a house cricket and a camel cricket?

House crickets are often brown with wings and are the ones that chirp. Camel crickets, sometimes called spider crickets, have a hump-backed look, long legs, and usually do not sing. Both like damp, dark rooms.

Can crickets damage a house?

They are mainly a nuisance. In large numbers they can nibble fabric, paper, or plants, and the chirping can be hard to sleep through. The bigger issue is the moisture or entry gap that let them in.

When should I call a professional for crickets?

Call if crickets keep appearing in a basement or crawl space after you have dried and sealed the area, or if you cannot reach the space safely yourself.
