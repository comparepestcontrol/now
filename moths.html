How to Get Rid of Pantry Moths in the Kitchen

Pantry moths in the kitchen usually start in a package, not in a wall. The insect homeowners see most often is the Indian meal moth. The adult is a small moth with a two-tone wing: lighter near the body and reddish brown or copper on the outer half. The damage, though, is done by the larvae. They feed in dry goods and leave silk webbing in flour, cereal, cornmeal, nuts, dried fruit, chocolate, spices, bird seed, and pet food.

Clothes moths are a different problem. They infest wool, fur, and other animal-fiber items, usually in a closet or storage box, not a bag of rice. If the moths are flying in the kitchen and you find webbing in food, treat it as a pantry issue. Using a closet-style approach will not solve it.

How to find the source

Empty every cabinet, pantry shelf, and countertop basket that holds dry food. Check the back row, the barely used baking supplies, a bag of flour that has been open for months, and the pet-food bin. Look in bird seed, decorative dried gourds, and bulk bags from a store. Open packages and look for living larvae, clumped food, webbing, or small cocoons in the corners of the box or bag.

Larvae also leave the food to pupate. Inspect the shelf above the food, the cabinet hinges, the junction where the wall meets the ceiling, and the undersides of shelves. Small cocoons can sit in those cracks even after the infested bag is gone. If moths are flying in a room that has no food, follow them back to the kitchen or check a nearby pantry, garage, or pet-food storage area.

Pheromone traps designed for pantry moths can confirm that adults are still present and show you which room is most active. Hang them away from windows and from food-prep surfaces, and follow the package directions. They are a monitor, not a complete solution.

What you can do yourself

Remove infested food and clean thoroughly

Bag heavily infested food, seal it, and put it in an outdoor trash can. If you prefer to keep an item that may only have been exposed, you can freeze it for at least four days at a household freezer temperature to kill eggs and larvae, then store it in a container with a tight lid. Do not pour infested food into a kitchen trash can and leave it indoors.

Vacuum shelves, cracks, corners, and the wall-ceiling junction, then wipe the shelves with soapy water. Empty the vacuum outside. Pull out every liner and every rarely moved appliance shelf in the pantry. This cleaning step is what removes cocoons that would otherwise turn into a new flight of moths a week later.

Store food so a new package cannot restart the problem

Move flour, cereal, grains, nuts, dried fruit, and pet food into glass or sturdy plastic containers with tight lids. Ordinary thin bags and cardboard boxes are not enough. Use older items first, and inspect new bulk purchases before they go on the shelf. Avoid spraying insecticide inside a pantry or on shelves that hold food. It is not needed if the source is gone, and it does not belong near dishes or groceries.

Keep the pheromone traps up for a few weeks after the cleanup. If they stay empty and you no longer see larvae, the source is likely gone. If adults keep appearing, you missed a package or a pupation site. Check again, including pet food in a garage or a bag of seed in a hall closet.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if you cannot find the source after a careful search, if moths return after cleaning and tight storage, or if the problem seems to involve a multi-unit building or several rooms at once. A technician can help locate hidden pupation sites and tell you whether you are dealing with pantry moths or another household moth.

If the insects are in a closet and you have not found any sign in food, say so when you call. That points toward a fabric moth problem, which needs a different inspection of wool, stored clothing, and undisturbed carpet or furniture.

Frequently asked questions

What do pantry moths look like?

The adult Indian meal moth is a small moth with pale forewings that turn reddish brown or copper on the outer half. In the kitchen you may also find silk webbing in flour, cereal, nuts, or pet food, and cream-colored larvae in those foods.

Do I have to throw out all of my food?

You do not have to discard every item, but you should inspect each dry-goods package, including pet food, bird seed, spices, and items in the back of the cabinet. Infested food should be bagged and thrown away or frozen long enough to kill all stages.

Do pheromone traps solve a pantry moth problem?

Traps catch adult males and help you confirm that moths are still present, but they will not end an infestation if the food source stays on the shelf. Finding and removing that source is the step that matters most.

When should I call a professional for pantry moths?

Call if you cannot find the source after a thorough cabinet search, if moths return after you have cleaned and stored food in tight containers, or if the problem seems to involve several rooms or a shared building.
