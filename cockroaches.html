How to Get Rid of Cockroaches in the Kitchen

A cockroach in the kitchen is unsettling, and it is also a clue. These insects come for food, water, and tight hiding places. In many U.S. homes the species that becomes a lasting kitchen problem is the German cockroach, a small tan insect with two dark bars on its back. Larger American cockroaches are more often tied to basements, drains, and utility areas, though they can wander into a kitchen as well.

Getting rid of them takes patience. The insects you see at night are only part of the population. The rest are in cracks behind the stove, under the sink, or in cabinet hinges. A spray that kills what runs across the floor usually leaves the hiding insects in place, and some products can scatter them into new rooms.

How to tell if you have a kitchen infestation

Look for live insects after dark with a flashlight, especially behind the stove, refrigerator, and microwave. Also look for pepper-like droppings in cabinet corners, shed skins, and small egg cases. A musty odor can appear when numbers are high. Daytime sightings often mean the hiding spots are crowded.

Sticky monitors placed along the back edge of a counter, under the sink, and behind appliances will tell you where activity is heaviest. Check them every few days. That map is more useful than guessing.

German cockroaches usually stay close to the kitchen and bathroom, where food and water are reliable. If you only see a large cockroach near a floor drain or a basement door, you may have a different species moving in from elsewhere in the building. The cleanup steps below still help, but the treatment plan may need to include those other areas.

What you can do yourself

Take away food and water

Store food, including pet food and snacks, in containers with tight lids. Do not leave dishes in the sink, and wipe crumbs from counters, the stovetop, and the floor each night. Empty the trash and rinse bottles and cans. Fix dripping faucets and sweating pipes. Dry the sink before bed, and run the exhaust fan after you cook or wash dishes so the room does not stay humid.

Pull out the stove and refrigerator and clean the floor, the sides of the appliances, and any drip trays. Grease and crumbs in those gaps support a hidden population. Wash cabinet interiors, including the corners and the undersides of shelves, and take out rarely used items so you can see droppings or egg cases.

Use bait and monitors in the hiding places

Gel bait placed in small dabs in cracks, under the sink, behind appliances, and along cabinet hinges works because cockroaches hide there and share food. Follow the label. Use small placements and refresh them if they dry out or are eaten. Keep bait away from food-prep surfaces, children, and pets. A light dust of a product labeled for cockroach cracks and voids can help in wall gaps, but do not puff dust into the air or onto dishes.

Avoid making a fogger or bomb your first step. Those products often fail to reach insects deep in cracks and can drive them into new rooms. A heavy spray on baseboards has the same problem if the insects are behind the refrigerator.

Keep the monitors in place after you bait. If you stop catching cockroaches and you no longer see droppings in cleaned cabinets, you are on the right track. If catches stay steady, you have either missed a food source or the population is large enough that a technician should take over.

When to call a professional

Call a local pest control provider if you see cockroaches during the day, if several rooms are involved, if you live in a multi-unit building where insects can move through shared walls, or if careful cleaning and baiting have not reduced activity after one to two weeks. German cockroaches reproduce quickly, and an established population is hard to finish with store products alone.

A licensed technician can confirm the species, treat wall voids and appliance gaps more thoroughly, and set a follow-up plan. Ask what preparation you need to do, which areas they will treat, and how they will check results. If you rent, tell your landlord as well, because the problem may not start and end in your unit.

Frequently asked questions

Is one cockroach in the kitchen a problem?

A single cockroach can be a wanderer, but kitchen species such as the German cockroach rarely travel alone for long. Put out a sticky monitor and inspect at night before you assume it is a one-time sighting.

Do foggers get rid of cockroaches?

Total-release foggers often scatter cockroaches into walls and neighboring rooms and do little to the insects hidden in cracks. Gel bait and sanitation are more reliable starting points.

Where do kitchen cockroaches hide during the day?

They hide in tight, dark, warm gaps: behind the stove and refrigerator, in cabinet hinges, under the sink, around the dishwasher, and in cracks along the backsplash or floor.

When should I call a professional for cockroaches?

Call if you see cockroaches in the daytime, find droppings or egg cases in several cabinets, or still catch them on monitors after you have cleaned and baited for one to two weeks.
